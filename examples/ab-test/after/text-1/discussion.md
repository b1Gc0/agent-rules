The THG enhancement is consistent with quasi-BIC-mediated pump-field confinement within the crystalline silicon nanodisks, which strengthens the third-order nonlinear polarization through its cubic field dependence. [Liu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.nanolett.5c00146) This mechanism links resonant energy storage at 1560 nm to emission at 520 nm. The measured efficiency of 2.1 × 10⁻⁶ is approximately 8.6-fold below the simulated 1.8 × 10⁻⁵, accompanied by a Q reduction from 1200 to 310. Incorporating an 8° sidewall inclination lowers the simulated Q to approximately 400, supporting geometric deviations as a plausible contributor to reduced resonant enhancement. However, this inclination was measured on a separate cleaved test sample; its relevance to the optically characterized device requires direct verification. Moreover, Q alone cannot quantitatively explain the efficiency deficit, because pump coupling, spectral overlap and harmonic outcoupling also determine the measured conversion. Fabrication disorder and differences between simulated and experimental illumination remain possible additional contributors. At 1 GW/cm², the measured efficiency exceeds the reported amorphous-silicon value of 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ at 3 GW/cm² [reference needed]. This comparison is encouraging, but establishing an intrinsic advantage requires matched excitation conditions and collection efficiencies. Measurements on one device under x-polarized pumping establish performance only for that configuration; device-to-device reproducibility and polarization dependence remain to be assessed.
