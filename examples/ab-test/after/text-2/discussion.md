The THG enhancement in the crystalline-silicon nanodisk array on sapphire is consistent with quasi-BIC-mediated pump-field buildup at 1560 nm. The cubic dependence of the nonlinear polarization on the local pump field amplifies the source driving emission at 520 nm. The measured efficiency of 2.1 × 10⁻⁶ at 1 GW/cm² is approximately 8.6-fold below the simulated 1.8 × 10⁻⁵, alongside a Q reduction from 1200 to 310. Incorporating an 8° sidewall inclination lowers the simulated Q to approximately 400, supporting fabrication-induced geometric deviations as a plausible contributor to resonance degradation. However, the inclination was measured on a separate cleaved test specimen, leaving its relevance to the optically measured device unverified. Moreover, Q alone cannot determine THG efficiency, because resonant excitation depends on the balance between radiative coupling and internal losses ([Koshelev et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05402)). Quantitative reconciliation also requires assessing pump–resonance spectral overlap, harmonic absorption and collection efficiency. The measured efficiency exceeds the reported amorphous-silicon value of 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ at 3 GW/cm² [reference needed], despite the lower incident intensity. Establishing a material-specific advantage requires comparable pulse conditions and efficiency definitions, since perturbative THG efficiency scales quadratically with pump intensity. Measurements currently establish performance for one device under x-polarized excitation; reproducibility and polarization dependence remain to be characterized.
