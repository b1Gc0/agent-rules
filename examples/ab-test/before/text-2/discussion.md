The enhanced third-harmonic generation (THG) is consistent with quasi-BIC-mediated pump-field buildup in the crystalline-silicon nanodisks. At 1560 nm, resonant confinement strengthens the third-order nonlinear polarization, which depends cubically on the local pump field and drives emission at 520 nm. However, conversion depends on excitation coupling and harmonic extraction as well as Q, as emphasized by [critical-coupling analyses of nonlinear metasurfaces](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsphotonics.9b00700). The measured efficiency of 2.1 × 10⁻⁶ is approximately 8.6 times below the simulated 1.8 × 10⁻⁵, accompanying a Q reduction from 1200 to 310. Incorporating an 8° sidewall tilt reduces the simulated Q to approximately 400, suggesting that fabrication geometry could contribute substantially to resonance degradation. Nevertheless, the tilt was measured on a separate cleaved test sample; its relevance to the measured device, and its quantitative contribution to the efficiency deficit, remain unverified. Pump–resonance mismatch, additional losses and collection differences could also contribute, but are not established here. The efficiency exceeds the reported amorphous-silicon value of 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ at a lower peak intensity (1 versus 3 GW/cm²) [reference needed]. This comparison does not establish an intrinsic material advantage without consistent excitation and collection conditions. Measurements on one device with x-polarized pumping likewise leave device-to-device reproducibility and polarization dependence unresolved.
