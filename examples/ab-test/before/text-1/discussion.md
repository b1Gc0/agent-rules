The enhanced third-harmonic generation (THG) is consistent with quasi-BIC-mediated pump-field confinement in the crystalline silicon nanodisks on sapphire. Resonant energy accumulation at 1560 nm strengthens the third-order nonlinear polarization, whose cubic dependence on the local field promotes emission at 520 nm. However, conversion depends on excitation coupling and harmonic extraction as well as Q ([Koshelev et al.](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsphotonics.9b00700)). The measured efficiency of 2.1 × 10⁻⁶ at 1 GW/cm² is approximately 8.6-fold below the simulated 1.8 × 10⁻⁵, accompanied by a Q reduction from 1200 to 310. Incorporating an 8° sidewall tilt reduces the simulated Q to approximately 400, identifying taper as a plausible contributor. Nevertheless, the tilt was measured on a separate cleaved test sample, so its representativeness for the measured device remains unverified; agreement in Q alone does not quantitatively explain the efficiency deficit. Additional contributions could include fabrication disorder, pump–resonance spectral mismatch, and differences in collection efficiency. The measured efficiency exceeds the reported amorphous-silicon value of 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ at 3 GW/cm² [reference needed]. However, without matched excitation and collection conditions, this comparison cannot establish an intrinsic advantage of crystallinity. Measurements on one device with x-polarized excitation also leave reproducibility and polarization dependence unresolved, motivating correlated structural and optical characterization across multiple devices.
